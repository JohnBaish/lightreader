## About these figures

(Also shown at the foot of the page once a postcode is chosen.)

**Daylight:** The sun is up: from sunrise to sunset, when the top of the sun meets a flat horizon.

**Half-light** (civil twilight): The sun is just below the horizon, up to 6° down. Enough light to be out and about.

**Dark** (nautical twilight): The sun is 6° to 12° below the horizon. Too dark for most things outdoors without a light.

**Very dark** (astronomical twilight): The sun is 12° to 18° below the horizon. A faint glow may remain low in the sky.

**True night:** The sun is more than 18° below the horizon, and the sky is as dark as it gets.

The times are worked out in your browser for the middle of your postcode district, with Astronomy Engine, a free astronomy library. They're in UK clock time (GMT or BST), rounded to the nearest minute. Sunrise and sunset allow for the way the air bends the light near the horizon.

They're accurate to within a minute or so of the published times. They assume a flat, open horizon: real hills, buildings and the weather aren't taken into account, and moving about 10 miles east or west changes the times by about a minute.

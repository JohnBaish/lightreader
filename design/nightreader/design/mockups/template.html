The sky tonight

    Where to look

    Facing south, with east on the left and west on the right. The towns along the horizon are where they really lie from here. Slide through the night.

      Hold a fist out at arm’s length: it covers about 10° of sky. Stack fists up from the horizon to find the height.

      The year in darkness

      Every night from tonight for a year, dusk at the top and dawn at the bottom. The pale slanting bands are moonlight.

      Moonlight in the night

      Small discs along the top mark each full Moon, and rings each new Moon. The steps in March and October are the clocks changing.

      From May 23rd to July 19th the sky over Reading never reaches true night, so those weeks are measured by “very dark” instead.

      Mock-up for John. Every figure is real, worked out for the middle of RG1 with Astronomy Engine, as Lightreader’s are. All wording is draft.

      Draft rule for “in view”: the sky at least in half-light (the Sun 6° or more below the horizon) and the Moon or planet at least 15° up. The moonlight shading shows when the Moon is up and how full it is; it isn’t a measurement of brightness.
